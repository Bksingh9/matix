Under the Berne Convention, code is copyrighted the moment it is written and
publishing it changes nothing about that. GitHub's own terms say the same: a
public repository without a licence grants viewers the right to view and fork
it *within GitHub*, and nothing more. In particular, nobody may:

- run it as a service, paid or free
- redistribute it, modified or not
- reuse any part of it in another product

If you want to do any of those, ask.

## Why not open source

MindSharp's commercial plan is a paid subscription plus tutor, classroom and
white-label licences (`MONETISATION_PLAN.md`). A permissive licence would let
anyone launch the same product with none of those obligations, which is a poor
trade for a solo operator.

That is a decision, not an oversight, and it is reversible in one direction
only: this can be opened later, but a version published under an open licence
stays open forever.

## What this does not restrict

Reading the code, learning from it, and reporting problems are all welcome.

The dependencies are a different matter: each carries its own licence, and
those obligations apply regardless of anything above. `npm ls --all` and the
Gradle and CocoaPods manifests list them.
